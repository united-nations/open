# Trust-fund source options: UN-Habitat, ITC and UNODC

Sources inspected on 28 October 2026 for UNODC and 29 October 2026 for UN-Habitat
and ITC. Consolidated on 2 November 2026 from the two fund-source research
READMEs. The investigations did not change portal amounts, contributors,
hierarchies or financial-position data. The options below remain research
candidates unless separately integrated and documented.

## Findings at a glance

The question was whether public sources can extend entity-level expenditure
into individual fund accounts, including annual expenditure and financial
position. These are different requirements: project delivery, funding segments,
cash balances and IPSAS fund accounts cannot substitute for one another.

| Entity | Strongest public detail found | Limitation | Most useful next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| UN-Habitat | Five audited funding segments, plus public project information | Segments are not an inventory of individual fund codes; IATI amounts are cumulative | IPSAS fund/grant schedules and mapping of thematic funds to audited segments |
| ITC | Quarterly project expenditure in IATI and annual-report project delivery | Partial coverage; no verified allocation to funding windows or full fund positions | Complete 2024 annual-report appendices, year-end Trust Fund committee report and fund/grant trial balance |
| UNODC | Audited Drug/Crime segments, a candidate derived annual XB split, and some individual-fund statements | Current programme-level RB expense is not separately printed; pillar balance sheets include RB | Confirmation of the RB derivation and an IPSAS trial balance by fund/grant |

RB means regular budget; XB means extrabudgetary. IPSAS is the accounting basis
of the audited financial statements. UNSAS and budget-basis figures can have a
different recognition basis and scope.

## UN-Habitat

### What the audited reports support

[A/80/5/Add.9, Note 5, PDF pp. 80–83](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4090387/files/A_80_5_Add.9-EN.pdf#page=80)
reports five XB groups: Foundation non-earmarked, Foundation earmarked,
technical cooperation, programme support and employee benefits. They have
audited performance and position columns, but are funding groups rather than
five established individual trust-fund accounts.

In 2024 their gross expenses total **$188.704 million**. Intersegment
eliminations of **$16.059 million** produce the existing non-RB total of
**$172.645 million**. Adding these group expenses to that total would double count.
The report says grants are managed within trust funds with their own terms of
reference (PDF p. 18, paragraph 22); it does not provide a complete fund/grant
mapping.

The separate [2024 Chapter IV supplement](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4090387/files/A_80_5_Add-9-ch-IV.pdf?version=1)
provides explanations and repeats segment information, without a finer account
inventory. Table IV.6 on supplement p. 12 confirms Foundation non-earmarked
revenue of **$6.227 million**, expenditure of **$3.743 million** and net assets of
**$9.149 million**, consistent with the existing extraction. No additional
individual-account schedule was found in the inspected Digital Library listing.

## International Trade Centre

### Why audited statements do not provide the desired split

[A/80/5 (Vol. III), PDF p. 50, paragraph 77](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4090394/files/A_80_5_%28Vol._III%29-EN.pdf#page=50)
treats ITC as one IPSAS operating segment. Statement II therefore does not split
Window I, Window II and programme support. The
[separate Chapter IV annex, p. 14](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4090394/files/A_80_5_Vol-III-ch-IV.pdf?version=1#page=14)
covers write-offs, fraud and ex gratia payments, not a fund-account inventory.

Several figures must not be mistaken for that inventory:

- Trust Fund reserves of **$23.748 million** and programme-support reserves of
  **$2.020 million** (PDF p. 71) are reserve components, not whole-fund balances.
- Employee-benefit liabilities by General Fund/PSC/XB (PDF p. 69) are only one
  liability component, not complete financial positions.
- Net voluntary contributions and transfers of **$126.382 million** (PDF p. 73)
  are revenue, not fund-specific expenditure.
- Statement V's **$43.533 million** RB expense is on the budget basis. Subtracting
  it from audited Statement II expenses would mix accounting bases. The existing
  **$117.3 million** XB figure remains reported gross delivery.

### Candidate: quarterly project expenditure

The [first-party IATI download](https://iatidata.intracen.org/IatiActivities.xml)
inspected was generated on **9 July 2026**, contained 238 activities and matched
the [IATI bulk copy](https://bulk-data.iatistandard.org/itc/itc-2018.xml)
byte-for-byte. The bulk filename is not a transaction-year restriction.

The investigation selected transaction type **4, expenditure**, grouped by
transaction year, verified USD currency and retained negative adjustments. It
excluded incoming funds, commitments and disbursements. Descriptions identify
quarterly expenditure; no duplicate project/quarter expenditure keys were found.

| Year | Project expenditure | Projects | Quarterly records | Negative entries, signed sum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | $90,727,342 | 173 | 574 | −$666,218 |
| 2024 | $95,721,622 | 159 | 564 | −$552,223 |

Examples of 2024 annual project amounts are Pakistan GRASP (B466),
**$5,029,657**, and MARKUP II (C189), **$8,995,973**. Project titles and reported
funders provide context, not donor/fund allocation of these expenses. A project
can combine Window I/BDF and external funding.

Coverage and accounting compatibility remain unverified. These sums do not equal
reported gross XB delivery of **$112.2 million in 2023** and **$117.3 million in
2024**. The [2024 Operational Plan, printed p. 26](https://www.intracen.org/file/opplan2024pdfpdf-0)
includes an IATI coverage KPI for TRTA projects, with a 90% target and 91%
preliminary 2023 actual. It does not reconcile these particular sums. The gap
must not be turned into a residual fund or attributed to programme support.

### Follow-up

The [2023 annual-report Appendix I, PDF pp. 4–43](https://www.intracen.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/ITC_Appendices-AR23_20240529_webpages.pdf#page=4)
has project identifiers, funders, budgets and actual delivery. Country/region
allocations can repeat a project, so counting each whole-project amount again
would duplicate delivery. An official equivalent for 2024 was not retrieved;
figures from an indexed third-party copy were not used.

The [official JAG notice](https://unctad.org/system/files/information-document/jag-notification_2025d109_en.pdf)
identifies the 2024 annual report as **ITC/AG(LIX)/298**. Seek that report's full
appendices and the **2024 year-end Consultative Committee of the ITC Trust Fund
(CCITF) report**, especially Window I/II income, delivery, carry-forward and
closing-balance schedules. Older reports contain such tables, but a current
equivalent was not verified. For complete IPSAS positions, request the year-end
trial balance by fund/grant. Cloudflare/403 responses during retrieval do not
prove that the documents are restricted. No external request was sent.

## UNODC

### Relationship between segments and funds

[A/76/5/Add.10, PDF p. 53, paragraph 3](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/3936081/files/A_76_5_Add.10-EN.pdf#page=53)
explicitly directs readers to segment reporting for results of the Fund of the
United Nations International Drug Control Programme and the United Nations
Crime Prevention and Criminal Justice Fund. This is stronger evidence of a fund
relationship than the names “Drug” and “Crime” alone.

[ST/SGB/2015/4, Annex III](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&s=ST%2FSGB%2F2015%2F4&t=pdf)
defines the two Funds (rule 401.1), establishes general-purpose, special-purpose
and programme-support accounts for each (404.1), permits investment-income
credits (404.2), and requires distinguishing the Funds in the accounts (406.2).
Those rules establish an accounting structure, not public access to its ledger.

The current [A/80/5/Add.10, PDF p. 63, paragraph 84](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4090388/files/A_80_5_Add.10-EN.pdf#page=63)
identifies Drug and Crime pillars, with UNICRI included in Crime. Non-earmarked
activities are allocated using the programmes' performance ratio.

### Candidate: derived annual XB expenses by programme

The report recognizes the yearly utilized RB allocation as revenue (PDF p. 51,
paragraph 19). Note 18, PDF p. 82, shows equal RB revenue and expenditure for
UNODC overall, but does not print RB expenditure separately for each programme.
Subtracting each programme's reported utilized RB allocation from its total
expenses yields:

| Year | Programme | Total expenses | RB allocation | Derived XB expenses |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024 | Drug | $141.821m | $17.322m | $124.499m |
| 2024 | Crime, including UNICRI | $342.977m | $19.858m | $323.119m |
| 2023 | Drug | $133.032m | $15.836m | $117.196m |
| 2023 | Crime, including UNICRI | $318.480m | $18.821m | $299.659m |

Inputs are printed on [2024 PDF p. 66](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4090388/files/A_80_5_Add.10-EN.pdf#page=66)
and [2023 PDF p. 80](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4059190/files/A_79_5_Add.10-EN.pdf#page=80).
The derived sums match the audited XB controls of **$447.618 million** and
**$416.855 million**. No UNSAS proportions or ARWO amounts were used.

**The remaining assumption is column-level equality of utilized RB allocation
and RB expense.** The policy and historical
[A/69/5/Add.10, printed p. 87, Note 3(a)(i)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/777885/files/A_69_5_Add.10-EN.pdf)
support that treatment, but overall arithmetic agreement does not prove it for
each 2023/2024 column. This is a candidate derivation, not newly discovered
printed individual-fund accounts. RB-inclusive pillar balance sheets also remain
unsuitable as pure fund positions: assets and liabilities cannot be stripped of
RB without an actual stock allocation.

### Follow-up

The [official Umoja report inventory](https://umoja.un.org/fr/umoja-reporting/all)
documents **ZGLTRIALBAL**, runnable by Business Area, Fund, Grant and Segment.
The [BI Trial Balance report](https://umoja.un.org/ru/node/860) is another route
to opening balances, annual movements and closing balances. Their existence
does not establish a complete public UNODC export or access to one. Certified
donor reports generally use modified cash reporting and are not substitutes for
an IPSAS trial balance.

Seek from UNODC/UNOV Finance the 2023 and 2024 year-end IPSAS trial balance or
equivalent schedule by fund code/name and grant, retaining GL account, opening
balance, annual movements and closing balance. Request the mapping to Drug and
Crime Funds and identification of RB, programme-support and elimination entries.
Ask explicitly whether utilized RB allocation equals expense in each Note 3
programme column. No external request was sent.

## Retention and interpretation

The original research verified source vintages separately from retrieval dates
and retained signed project-level observations. This consolidated note preserves
the useful results and public source locations; it does not require retaining
the downloaded XML/PDF files, derived CSVs, manifests or verification scripts.
Before adopting any candidate, retrieve its relevant vintage again and reconcile
grain, period, accounting basis and eliminations to the published control.
